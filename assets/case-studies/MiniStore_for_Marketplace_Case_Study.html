01 — The Insight

Retailers Were Buying in Bulk. We Just Weren’t Rewarding It.

The marketplace was built for one retailer buying for one counter. What we found was different: retailers were buying for their own stores in bulk, because our prices were better than their local supply.

THE BEHAVIOUR

A wholesale use case hiding inside a retail marketplace

  • Retailers ran tier 2 and tier 3 stores, and our marketplace was cheaper than their usual sources.
  • So they bought in bulk, stored the stock, and resold it — using the platform as a supplier.
  • None of this was designed for: a bulk order cost the same per unit as a single item.
THE GAP

No reason to grow the basket

With no price break for buying more, there was nothing nudging a retailer to add one more brand’s worth of stock to an order.

THE OPPORTUNITY

Brands wanted volume too

Bulk orders mean bigger, predictable volume for a brand — something we could take to brand partners in exchange for better prices at higher order values.

02 — The Solution

MiniStore: Bulk Buying, Priced Like Bulk Buying

A bulk-discount layer on the existing marketplace. Partner brands set discount slabs by cart value; the cart shows the retailer how close they are to the next slab; and the discount follows the order everywhere it goes.

🏷️

Brand-level slabs

Higher cart value of a brand, higher flat discount.
🛒

Nudged in the cart

One clear prompt: how much more to add, and what it unlocks.
🧾

Transparent pricing

Discount shown as a separate line item, everywhere.
⚙️

Backend-configured

Schemes uploaded from a sheet — fast to launch and change.

For the retailer

A real price break for the way they already buy. The cart tells them exactly how much more of a brand to add to reach the next discount, and every invoice and order record shows what they saved.

For brands & sellers

Bigger, predictable orders from a retailer network that was already buying from them. The seller panel and order reports show the discount as its own line, so every order reconciles cleanly.

03 — Scoping Phase 1

What I Deliberately Left Out of the First Release

Phase 1 had one job: prove that retailers would grow their baskets for a bulk discount. Everything that didn’t serve that waited.

Deferred

Self-serve scheme setup in the Seller Panel

Sellers creating and editing their own discount slabs.

Phase 1: schemes configured only on the backend, from a sheet — no seller-facing UI to build first.

Deferred

A dedicated MiniStore entry point

A new section on the landing page, or promotion on product pages.

Phase 1: promoted through banners only; no landing-page entry point, no product-page promotion.

Deferred

Different discounts per product

A separate discount on every SKU.

Phase 1: one flat cart discount per brand slab. SKU-level discounts became Phase 2.

Shipped — Phase 1

Brand-level flat discounts, prompted in the cart, applied as a line item

The smallest version that changes buying behaviour end to end: cart prompt, discounted checkout, order history, seller panel and order reports — with call support helping retailers place their first bulk orders.

Enough to test the behaviour with real money, without building the configuration and discovery layers first.

04 — Retailer Flow

How a Bulk Order Works, Step by Step

01

Discovers it through a banner

MiniStore is promoted through banners only — the retailer’s existing home and browse experience stays unchanged.

02

Adds products to the cart, across any brand

Nothing changes about browsing or adding to cart. Products from every brand can sit in the same cart.

03

Sees the prompt on the cart page

“Add products worth ₹X more of <Brand> to avail Y% discount” — the only place the prompt appears.

04

Crosses the slab — the discount applies

Once that brand’s cart value reaches a slab, its flat discount is applied, shown in the cart summary with the final cart value.

05

Pays the discounted price

The retailer proceeds to payment as usual; the order is placed at the discounted price.

06

Sees the saving in order history

The discount shows as its own line item against the order — and the same line flows to the seller panel and order reports.

    05 — Feature by Feature

    What This Looked Like, Shipped

    05.1 · SCHEME ENGINE

    Discount Slabs, Configured per Brand

    A new scheme table holds every slab: a cart-value range, the brand it applies to, and the flat cart discount. The cart checks each brand’s subtotal against its slabs and applies the matching one.

    • Brand-level, not cart-level — slabs are calculated on each brand’s share of the cart, so one brand’s discount never depends on another’s products.
    • Uploaded from a sheet, configured on the backend — new partners and slabs could go live without a release.
    • Every applied discount carries its Scheme ID, so any order can be traced back to the rule that priced it.

    05.2 · CART PROMPT

    One Nudge, in One Place

    On the cart page — and only there — the retailer sees how far they are from the next slab: “Add products worth ₹X more of <Brand> to avail Y% discount.”

    • It appears at the moment the retailer is deciding how big the order should be.
    • It turns an abstract scheme into a concrete number — “₹10,000 more”.
    • Keeping it to the cart meant no changes to browsing, product pages or the home screen in Phase 1.

    05.3 · CART SUMMARY & PRICING

    The Discount as Its Own Line Item

    The cart summary shows the discount applied on that seller and the final cart value, and the retailer pays the discounted amount. The discount never disappears into the price — it stays a separate line.

    • Retailers see exactly what buying in bulk saved them.
    • The discount is calculated on the selling price (including GST), so the final debit matches what the retailer saw in the cart.
    • Phase 2 adds rounding of the final price after discount.

    Worked example · 5% slab

    05.4 · ORDERS, SELLERS & REPORTING

    The Discount Follows the Order Everywhere

    A discount that shows in the cart but not in the books creates support tickets and reconciliation fights. So the same line item carries through every downstream view.

    • Order history — the discount appears as a separate line on the prices applied.
    • Seller panel — sellers see the discount as its own line item on each order.
    • Order punch-in — each order is recorded with SP, discount and final price.
    • Order list download — four new columns, so finance and brand partners can reconcile without asking.

    06 — Business Sign-Off

    The Rules We Agreed Before Launch

    Each of these was confirmed with the business before build, so engineering, sellers and support were working to the same version.

    01
    Configured on the backend only. Schemes come from the agreed sheet — not from the Seller Panel.
    02
    Banners only, no new entry point. No MiniStore section and no product-page promotion in this release.
    03
    Flat discounts, as per the sheet. One discount per brand slab.
    04
    Prompts on the cart page only. “Add products worth ₹X more of <Brand> to avail Y% discount.”
    05
    Discount as a separate line item — through checkout and everything after it.
    06
    Orders report records SP, discount and final price for every order.
    07
    Call support for first bulk orders. In the first release, the support team helped retailers place bulk orders.

    08 — Phase 2

    From Brand Slabs to SKU-Wise Discounts

    Once the brand-level behaviour was proven, Phase 2 let each product carry its own discount within a scheme.

    Phase 2 · SKU-wise

    Each SKU Mapped to a Scheme and a Discount

    A mapping table links SKU ID → Scheme ID → discount, so the same product can earn a different discount in a different slab.

    • Mixed discounts, one message — when products with different discounts sit in the cart, the prompt becomes “Add a few products worth ₹X more of <Brand> to avail attractive discounts.”
    • Discounts shown against each product, as they already were, with the combined discount added at the end.
    • Final price rounded off after the discount is applied.
