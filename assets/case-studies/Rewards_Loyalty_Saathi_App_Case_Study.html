00 — Context

Why the Module Changed in the First Place

PayNearby's Banking Correspondent network runs on retail counters — a retailer facilitates every transaction for a customer and earns commission on it. Saathi flipped that: the customer transacts on the app directly, and the retailer earns commission for referring and enabling them.

Intent was good — free up the retailer's counter and make the customer self-sufficient. But asking someone to change a habit overnight, with nothing new in it for them, was always going to be the hard part.
The app shift alone wasn't enough — this case study is about what had to be added to make the switch actually stick.

01 — The Problem

The Shift Didn't Stick

The intent behind Saathi was right. In practice, the customer side of the equation refused to move — and the retailer side lost the one thing that made the old model bearable: visibility.

CUSTOMER HABIT RESISTANCE

The customer wasn't ready to change how they transacted

  • No repeat transactions on Saathi once the novelty of a first try wore off.
  • New active customers kept appearing, but DAU/MAU kept dropping — activation without retention.
  • The same customer, days later, would be back at the counter transacting through the retailer on the PayNearby app — the exact behaviour Saathi was meant to replace.
  • Retailer interviews and a structured survey both confirmed this wasn't a hunch — it was the dominant pattern.
RETAILER VISIBILITY GAP

Retailers couldn't see their own earnings

No separate view into what a Saathi customer's transactions were earning them — which customers were active, which had gone quiet, or how commissions broke down customer by customer.

COMPOUNDING EFFECT

Two problems, feeding each other

A retailer with no visibility into Saathi earnings had no reason to push a customer toward the app — and a customer with no reward for switching had no reason to change.

02 — Discovery

How I Landed on This Problem

I didn't start with a solution in mind — I started by reading and listening, on both the original monthly-payout question and the wider habit-change problem.

01

Read the tickets

Three months of CRM tickets tagged 'rewards' / 'incentive' kept surfacing the same question: "Where is my incentive for last week's recharge?"

02

Checked the data

DAU/MAU for top-tier retailers dipped in the two weeks after each month-end payout — and separately, Saathi's own DAU/MAU curve kept flattening despite new signups.

03

Sat with retailers

Visited retail counters and watched how they checked earnings, what confused them, and — critically — watched the same customers walk back in to transact in person.

04

Talked to CRM & Growth

Understood how the existing month-end mechanism worked, where it was stretched thin, and ran a structured retailer survey to confirm the habit-resistance pattern.

03 — Validation

Confirming This Was Worth Solving

Three signals, all pointing the same way — that's when I knew this wasn't a one-off complaint.

01
Behaviour dipped, predictably. The same DAU/MAU dip, every month, in the two weeks after payout — across cohorts, not a coincidence.
02
Tickets and surveys agreed. "Where's my reward" was one of the most repeated CRM themes, and the retailer survey independently confirmed customers reverting to the old, retailer-assisted flow.
03
Retailers said it themselves. Unprompted, more than one retailer said they'd rather get paid "a little, right away" than "a lot, but late" — and that a customer with no reason to return to the app simply wouldn't.
With that evidence in hand, I took it to the CRM and Growth leads — and we agreed this was worth prioritising ahead of other asks that quarter.

04 — Options Considered

What I Weighed Before Landing on Rewards

Three directions were on the table. Each fixed a piece of the problem — none fixed both sides of it at once.

Option A

Redesign the monthly payout to run weekly

Simpler to ship — just a faster version of the existing mechanism.

Still delayed, still invisible until the day it lands. Doesn't touch customer habit at all.

Option B

Push notification nudges on payout day

Cheap to build, reminds retailers to check their earnings.

Fixes awareness, not the underlying disconnect between action and reward — and does nothing for the customer side.

Option C

Pay retailers only for new customer acquisition

Directly rewards the retailer for pushing the app to new customers.

Solves acquisition, not retention — the exact gap the data showed. Repeat usage still gets no incentive.

Chosen — Rewards & Loyalty Layer

A layer that rewards the transaction itself, for both sides

The only option that could make a customer want to transact on Saathi again, while giving the retailer a live reason to keep pushing them to do it — instead of solving one half of the loop and leaving the other broken.

Addresses habit resistance and visibility together, instead of trading one off against the other.

05 — The Solution

A Rewards & Loyalty Layer, Built for Two Audiences

Instead of one more monthly-payout tweak, I proposed a layer that sits on top of Saathi — pays the moment a transaction is earned, shows exactly why, and gives the retailer a live view of who's using the app and who isn't.

⚡

Instant, not monthly

Reward lands the same instant as the transaction.
🔍

Fully transparent

Every rupee traceable to the transaction that earned it.
🎁

A little bit of delight

Randomised rewards make checking the app worth doing.
📈

Built to scale

Runs quietly alongside the existing monthly system.

For the customer

Every transaction earns a randomised reward — cash, a coupon, or a "better luck next time" — so the app itself becomes worth opening again. The unpredictability is the hook that habit alone couldn't provide.

For the retailer

Full visibility into every Saathi customer's activity and commission, plus milestone-based incentives tied to how active their referred customers are — turning the retailer into someone actively motivating repeat usage.

Feature by Feature

What This Looked Like, Shipped

05.1

Real-Time Instant Credit

The heart of it. A qualifying Recharge or Mutual Fund transaction credits the retailer's main ledger the same instant — via the GDC API — confirmed right there on screen.

  • Puts the reward back next to the action that earned it.
  • The single biggest lever on repeat-transaction frequency.
  • Runs quietly alongside the existing monthly system — nothing legacy breaks.

Real-time instant credit — confirmed on screen the moment it lands

05.2

Gamified Scratch-Card Coupons

After a transaction, a scratch card reveals a randomised reward — Cash, a GrabOn Coupon, or Better Luck Next Time — with payout ratios tunable per campaign.

  • The unpredictability is the point — a moment of delight, not just a payout.
  • Gives a reason to open the app the same day, not just at month-end.
  • Ratio could be dialed per service without touching the core engine.

Scratch card reveal — the moment of delight after a transaction

05.3

Loyalty Balance & Redeem

One screen, one number. Vouchers and Incentives combine into a single Loyalty Balance, and one tap redeems it straight to the Main Ledger.

  • No more guessing — always know exactly what's been earned.
  • One-tap redemption meant fewer "how do I get my money" support calls.
  • A small ₹10 minimum-transfer rule kept settlement costs sane at scale.

Loyalty Balance — vouchers + incentives, one tap to redeem

05.4

Incentive History by Customer

Every rupee traceable back to the exact customer and transaction that earned it — mobile number, transaction count, per-service split, all in one view.

  • Retailers could finally see which customers were worth nurturing.
  • Nudged retailers to cross-sell AePS, Mutual Funds and GPA to the right people.
  • No reward ever showed up without a reason attached — that's what built trust.

Incentive History by Customer — every rupee traced to its source

05.5

Earnings & Redeem History

A running record of every past redemption — date, amount, Ledger ID, and a Vouchers vs. Incentives split — always there to refer back to.

  • Turned a one-time fix into something retailers trusted long-term.
  • Let retailers self-serve reconciliation instead of raising a ticket.
  • Ledger IDs gave CRM a clean trail whenever an escalation came in.

Earnings & Redeem History — a running, self-serve record

05.6

Search & Filter Incentives

For the busiest retailers, a simple search by mobile number or name, plus a filter by service — Saathi or PayNearby.

  • Kept the experience usable once a retailer had hundreds of entries.
  • A faster lookup meant a faster path from "I have a question" to "okay, I trust this."
  • Gave a cleaner data model to build service-level analytics on, later.

Search & Filter — built for retailers with hundreds of entries

06 — Proving It Worked

How I Validated the Solution

This didn't roll out to all 20,000+ retailers on day one — proof came first.

Pilot

Started with a smaller cohort

Ran instant-credit and the scratch-card flow across two regions, for four weeks, before touching the full base.

Control

Compared against a control group

Tracked repeat-transaction rate and DAU/MAU for the pilot against a matched group still on the old monthly cycle.

Iterate

Re-tuned the reward mix

The first scratch-card payout ratio was too conservative — retailers noticed. Re-tuned it with Growth before wider rollout.

Close the loop

Went back to the same retailers

Checked whether the shipped fix actually matched what they'd asked for during discovery.
